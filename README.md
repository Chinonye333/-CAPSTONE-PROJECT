### CAPSTONE PROJECT 6: NYC COLLISION ANALYSIS
#### Dataset 2: NYC Motor Vehicle Collision Analysis | Total Accidents: 216k
The NYC Motor Vehicle Collision dataset contains information about reported traffic collisions, including collision dates and times, locations, contributing factors, vehicle types, and the number of people injured or killed.
The analysis depicts accident frequency, temporal patterns, high-risk locations, and contributing factors to identify trends that may support road safety planning and targeted accident-prevention measures.

#### RESULTS:
- Q1. Accidents by month
Highest: March (about 23K, 10%) and January (21K, 10%), then February (19K, 9%).
Middle: about 17-18K (8%) for the rest of the year.
Lowest: December (15K, 7%).
March and January recorded the highest accident shares, followed by February.
November and December had the lowest displayed percentages.
The monthly distribution shows that accidents were reported throughout the year, with relatively small differences between most months.
Seasonal pattern: The analysis that accident frequency was relatively high during the beginning of the year and lower toward the end of the year.
 
- Q2. Friday recorded the highest number of accidents, at approximately 34,000.
Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Saturday each recorded approximately 31,000 accidents.
Monday recorded approximately 30,000 accidents.
Sunday recorded the lowest number, at approximately 28,000.
These findings suggest that accidents were more frequent toward the end of the working week, particularly on Friday.
Possible explanations include increased traffic volume, commuting activity, shopping trips, and social activities.

- Q3. Lafayette Avenue recorded the highest accident count among the streets displayed.
The street accounts for approximately 33.78% of the total accidents in the dataset. This makes it an important location for further investigation and potential road safety interventions.

- Q4. All accidents: “Unspecified” leads, but the top named factor is Driver Inattention/Distraction.
Fatal accidents: “Unspecified” is first. Among named causes, Unsafe Speed is the top (fatal accidents), then Driver Inattention, Failure to Yield and Traffic Control Disregarded.

#### Extra insights:
Unsafe Speed is the deadliest cause per accident. About 1.4% of its crashes were fatal (104 of 7,553), against 0.12% for Driver Inattention. Inattention causes the most crashes, but speed causes the most deaths relative to its frequency.

### CAPSTONE PROJECT 4: EMPLOYEE SURVEY RESPONSES
#### Dataset 2: Employee Survey Response analysis | Total Responses: 14,725
The Employee Survey Responses dataset contains employee feedback collected through survey questions, along with information about departments and organizational roles.
The dashboard provides insights into employees’ satisfaction, workplace experiences, perceptions of management, and organizational culture. The analysis focuses on the survey questions with the highest agreement and disagreement, trends across departments and roles, and recommendations for improving employee satisfaction.

#### Visualization and Analysis
![](https://github.com/Chinonye333/-CAPSTONE-PROJECT/blob/main/Employee%20Response%20-5.png)

### Analysis:
- Question 1: Which survey questions did respondents agree with or disagree with most?
- Question 2: Do you see any patterns or trends by department or role?
- Question 3: As an employer, what steps might you take to improve employee satisfaction based on the survey results?

#### Results:
- Q1. The highest agreement was recorded for “I know what is expected of me at work,” with 92% agreement and 8% disagreement. This suggests that most employees understand their responsibilities and what is expected of them in the workplace.
Additionally, 86% of respondents agreed that their supervisor or someone at work cares about them as individuals. This indicates a generally positive perception of supervisory support.

However, agreement with the statement about departmental inclusiveness was 83%, meaning that approximately 17% of respondents did not agree. This suggests that opportunities exist to strengthen inclusion and ensure that all employees feel valued.

The statement “I have a best friend at work” recorded the highest disagreement at 47%. Although 53% agreed, the relatively high disagreement suggests that many employees may not experience strong personal connections or close friendships at work.

Furthermore, 35% disagreed with the statement about receiving recognition in the previous seven days. This suggests that employee recognition may be an area requiring attention.
Job satisfaction also deserves attention. Although 76% of respondents agreed that they were satisfied with their jobs, 24% disagreed. This means that approximately one in four respondents did not express agreement with the statement.
- Q2. Most displayed departments recorded agreement levels between 74% and 85%. This suggests that positive perceptions of the workplace are relatively widespread across these departments.
The prosecuting department recorded high agreement: The department recorded approximately 85% agreement and 15% disagreement, making it one of the strongest performers in the displayed comparison.

District Court may require attention: Its displayed agreement level of 74% is lower than that of most other departments.
The Sheriff's department requires further investigation: The Analysis shows approximately 52% agreement and 48% disagreement. This is a substantially narrower gap than in the other displayed departments, this department should be prioritized for further investigation to identify possible concerns involving management, workload, recognition, or working conditions.
#### Additional Insight:
The differences between departments suggest that employee experiences may not be uniform across the organization.
Management should avoid applying the same intervention to every department. Instead, departments with lower agreement should be examined individually to identify the specific factors affecting their employees.
- Trends by employee role
The dashboard also compares responses across four employee categories: staff, directors, managers, and supervisors.
The chart shows that agreement and strong agreement are present categories.
Disagreement and strong disagreement also appear across the role, indicating that some employees have less positive workplace experiences.
#### RECOMMENDATION: 
- Q3.Based on the findings, I would recommend the following actions.
Improve employee recognition
The survey shows that 35% of respondents disagreed with the statement about receiving recognition in the previous seven days.
This suggests that employee recognition deserves attention.
Introduce a monthly employee recognition programme.
Encourage supervisors to acknowledge good performance regularly.
Recognize employees for teamwork, innovation, reliability, and outstanding service.
Ensure that recognition is fair and not limited to a small group of employees.
Expected outcome: Employees may feel more valued, appreciated, and motivated to contribute to organizational goals.

#### RECOMMENDATION & CONCLUSION 
Global Superstore should review its discount strategy, particularly in countries such as Nigeria, where excessive discounts may be contributing to losses. Discounts should be based on profit margins to ensure that sales generate sustainable profits.

The company should investigate products and subcategories with high shipping costs, especially Machines and Copiers in the United States. Negotiating better rates with shipping providers and optimising delivery methods could help reduce operational expenses.

The company should also tailor its pricing, product selection, and promotional activities to the performance of individual countries. Markets with strong profitability should be evaluated for growth opportunities, while underperforming markets should receive targeted attention.

The analysis shows that Global Superstore has opportunities to improve profitability through better discount management, shipping cost control, product optimisation, and customer retention. By using data-driven decisions to address loss-making areas and strengthen profitable operations, the company can improve financial performance and achieve more sustainable growth.
